# Advanced courses and coursework

Jonathan's coursework spans applied mathematics, statistics, machine learning,
economics, finance, optimization, business, and clinical data science. The
Rutgers courses below are upper-level undergraduate courses, while the Johns
Hopkins courses are graduate courses. Instructor and term details are not
included.

## Coursework overview

**Rutgers upper-level undergraduate courses:** Probability and Stochastic
Processes; Introduction to Computational Mathematics; Data Visualization;
Statistical Models; Managerial Economics; Economics of Labor; Health Economics;
Cost-Benefit Analysis; Inflation, Unemployment, and Public Policy; Applied Game
Theory; Economics of International Finance; Optimization and Spreadsheet
Modeling; Business Law; Management Accounting; Corporate Finance; Operations
Management; Business Valuation; Textual Analysis in Finance; Strategic
Management; Investment Analysis; Asset Pricing and Portfolio Analysis; and
Corporate Risk Management.

**Johns Hopkins graduate courses:** Computing for Applied Mathematics;
Introduction to Data Science; Bayesian Statistics; Time Series Analysis;
Probabilistic Machine Learning I and II; Elements of Statistical Learning I and
II; Nonparametric Bayesian Statistics; Nonlinear Optimization I and II; Machine
Learning I and II; Mathematics of Data Science; Statistical Theory; and
Generative AI in Clinical Data Science.

## Rutgers University–Camden

### Probability and Stochastic Processes

**Focus:** A mathematically rigorous introduction to probability and stochastic
modeling, including conditional probability, independence, random variables,
expectation, variance, laws of large numbers, limit theorems, and the long-run
behavior of Markov chains.

### Introduction to Computational Mathematics

**Focus:** Numerical methods for mathematical problems, including floating-point
representation, interpolation, root finding, numerical integration and
differentiation, and numerical optimization.

### Data Visualization

**Focus:** The data-science pipeline for transforming, exploring, and visualizing
data; publication-quality graphics; interactive exploratory tools; and
mathematical data transformations. Python and common data-science packages were
used extensively.

### Statistical Models

**Focus:** Multiple linear regression, estimation, hypothesis testing, regression
diagnostics, analysis of variance, ridge and lasso regularization, and
generalized linear models.

### Managerial Economics

**Focus:** Applying economic analysis to managerial decisions involving market
demand and supply, revenue, costs, profits, optimal pricing, capital budgeting,
and product-line analysis.

### Economics of Labor

**Focus:** Wage and employment determination, labor movements, unions, minimum
wage policy, income distribution, and applications of marginal-productivity
theory to labor markets.

### Health Economics

**Focus:** Economic analysis of health and healthcare, including demand for
health and medical care, insurance, physicians and nurses, hospitals,
pharmaceutical markets, payment systems, delivery systems, and government
regulation.

### Cost-Benefit Analysis

**Focus:** Cost-benefit and cost-effectiveness analysis for healthcare,
technology assessment, public policy, public administration, urban planning,
and environmental decisions. Topics included valuation in primary and
secondary markets, discounting, uncertainty, sensitivity analysis, option
value, and social discount rates.

### Inflation, Unemployment, and Public Policy

**Focus:** Causes and consequences of inflation and unemployment, the
inflation-unemployment trade-off, and stabilization policies such as
wage-price controls, wage indexation, and Keynesian measures.

### Applied Game Theory

**Focus:** Application and interpretation of sequential, simultaneous, repeated,
auction, and collective-action games, with attention to experimental evidence
and practical strategic decision-making.

### Economics of International Finance

**Focus:** Exchange-rate determination under flexible and fixed systems, spot and
forward currency markets, purchasing-power parity, interest-rate parity, and
changes in major currencies.

### Optimization and Spreadsheet Modeling

**Focus:** Mathematical and spreadsheet models for managerial decision-making,
optimization programming, sensitivity analysis, and decisions under
uncertainty. Spreadsheets were used extensively for modeling and computation.

### Business Law

**Focus:** Legal principles and procedures, foundations of contract law, and
case-based application of legal reasoning to business situations.

### Management Accounting

**Focus:** Managerial uses of accounting for organizational planning and control,
including cost behavior and classification, cost-volume-profit analysis,
budgeting, performance measurement, and cost control.

### Corporate Finance

**Focus:** Framing business finance problems, performing financial analysis,
evaluating alternatives, and making consequential financial-management
decisions.

### Operations Management

**Focus:** Managing the transformation of materials, labor, and capital into
products and services, including competitive operations strategy, performance
measurement, process improvement, long-term planning, and daily process
control.

### Business Valuation

**Focus:** Estimating enterprise value through company, industry, economic, and
financial analysis using the principal approaches to business valuation.

### Textual Analysis in Finance

**Focus:** Mining unstructured financial data from filings, earnings calls,
news, social media, corporate websites, and market sources using APIs and web
scraping. Methods included bag-of-words, sentiment analysis, embeddings, topic
models, BERT, GPT-assisted workflows, OpenAI embeddings, and fine-tuning with
Python and R.

### Strategic Management

**Focus:** Explaining differences in firm performance through external analysis
of opportunities and threats, internal analysis of resources and capabilities,
business-level and corporate-level strategy, socially responsible strategy,
and integration across business disciplines.

### Investment Analysis

**Focus:** Financial markets and instruments, allocation to risky assets,
efficient markets, behavioral finance, technical analysis, term structure,
fixed-income valuation, duration, convexity, and common-stock valuation.

### Asset Pricing and Portfolio Analysis

**Focus:** Mean-variance portfolio selection, the efficient frontier, the
Markowitz model, single- and multifactor models, capital asset pricing,
efficient markets, performance evaluation, active management, and international
diversification.

### Corporate Risk Management

**Focus:** Measuring and mitigating interest-rate, credit, market, liquidity,
foreign-exchange, off-balance-sheet, and operational risk through insurance,
hedging, and diversification.

## Johns Hopkins University

### Computing for Applied Mathematics

**Focus:** Python programming for applied mathematics and data-science problems,
with computational examples drawn from finance, data analysis, and the physical
sciences and extensive use of the scientific Python ecosystem.

### Introduction to Data Science

**Focus:** Theory and implementation of supervised and unsupervised learning,
including generalized linear regression, principal components, nearest-neighbor
and Bayesian classifiers, support vector machines, logistic regression, trees,
random forests, K-means, Gaussian mixtures, and Laplacian eigenmaps. Work used
Python, Jupyter notebooks, visualization, and introductory SQL.

### Bayesian Statistics

**Focus:** Applied Bayesian inference, prior and posterior distributions,
hierarchical, spatial, longitudinal, categorical, and missing-data models;
model checking and selection; and Markov chain Monte Carlo using R or MATLAB.
Additional topics included Gaussian processes and Dirichlet processes.

### Time Series Analysis

**Focus:** Time- and frequency-domain analysis, descriptive methods, regression,
trend and smoothing methods, forecasting, linear systems, serial correlation,
stationary processes, and spectral analysis.

### Probabilistic Machine Learning I

**Focus:** Directed and undirected probabilistic graphical models, latent and
temporal models, message passing, Markov chain Monte Carlo, variational
inference, and modern generative models including variational autoencoders,
generative adversarial networks, normalizing flows, and diffusion models.

### Probabilistic Machine Learning II

**Focus:** Advanced deep generative modeling and probabilistic inference,
including variational autoencoders, normalizing flows, diffusion and
stochastic-differential-equation models, flow matching, stochastic interpolants,
flow maps, neural samplers, Schrödinger bridges, stochastic control, adjoint
matching, conditional generation, and inverse-problem steering. Applications
included structural biology, molecular and materials design, and protein design.

### Elements of Statistical Learning

**Focus:** Mathematical and computational foundations of statistical learning,
including linear and logistic regression, lasso, kernel methods, support vector
machines, clustering, principal components, linear discriminant analysis,
expectation-maximization, deep learning, optimization, generalization,
bias-variance trade-offs, validation, and model selection.

### Elements of Statistical Learning II: Deep Learning

**Focus:** Mathematical foundations and implementation of neural networks,
backpropagation, gradient optimization, regularization, convolutional networks,
attention, transformers, graph neural networks, generative models,
representation learning, and latent-variable models.

### Nonparametric Bayesian Statistics

**Focus:** Advanced Bayesian modeling and computation for real-data problems,
including Gaussian processes, Dirichlet processes, Pólya trees, dependent
Dirichlet processes, and Indian buffet processes.

### Nonlinear Optimization I

**Focus:** Theory and algorithms for unconstrained nonlinear optimization,
including optimality conditions, steepest descent, Newton and quasi-Newton
methods, line searches, trust regions, adaptive cubic regularization,
least-squares problems, and conjugate-gradient methods.

### Nonlinear Optimization II

**Focus:** Constrained nonlinear optimization, including constrained optimality
conditions, projected-gradient and accelerated subspace methods, simplex and
interior-point linear programming, duality, penalty and augmented-Lagrangian
methods, sequential quadratic programming, and ADMM applications such as sparse
inverse covariance estimation, consensus, and compressed sensing.

### Machine Learning I

**Focus:** Theoretical and practical statistical learning foundations, including
inner-product spaces, reproducing kernels, optimization, generalization error,
Bayes estimators, bias-variance trade-offs, linear and kernel regression,
support vector machines, lasso, logistic regression, decision trees, and neural
networks.

### Machine Learning II

**Focus:** Generative statistical and artificial-intelligence methods, Markov
chains, Monte Carlo sampling, unsupervised learning, dimensionality reduction,
manifold learning, and clustering.

### Mathematics of Data Science

**Focus:** Mathematical tools for high-dimensional data, including concentration
of measure, sub-Gaussian vectors, empirical processes, Rademacher complexity,
random matrices, Johnson-Lindenstrauss dimension reduction, randomized numerical
linear algebra, compressed sensing, convex relaxation, spectral methods,
stochastic block models, nonconvex optimization, matrix sensing, and matrix
completion. Additional topics included information-theoretic lower bounds,
kernels, overparameterization, double descent, and Markov decision processes.

### Statistical Theory

**Focus:** Mathematical statistics, including distribution theory, exponential
families, sufficiency, least squares, maximum likelihood, uniformly minimum-
variance unbiased estimation, hypothesis testing, the Neyman-Pearson lemma,
likelihood ratios, general linear models, Gauss-Markov theory, simultaneous
inference, decision theory, Bayes and minimax procedures, goodness-of-fit,
nonparametric methods, and robust methods.

### Generative AI in Clinical Data Science

**Focus:** Generative-AI engineering and clinical data science, including prompt
engineering, retrieval-augmented generation, fine-tuning, autonomous-agent
architectures, survival and longitudinal analysis, clinical-trial design, and
real-world data. Project work centered on building and deploying agentic
biostatistics systems for clinical-research tasks.
